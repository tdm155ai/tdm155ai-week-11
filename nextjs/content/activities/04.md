An **NFC card** holds a short piece of data, usually a link, that a phone reads when you tap it. To **motivate the device** is to give it a reason to be there: design a game where the tap matters, so the game wouldn't work without the card.

**You need:** an NFC card, a phone that can read one, a partner, and paper. Read the NFC part of [Technology](/elements/technology) first.

## Do

1. **Tap a card.** What does a tap tell the game? A card in a place, at a time, in someone's hand.
2. **Read the card mechanics:** [nfc-app/MECHANICS.md](https://github.com/tdm155ai/tdm155ai-week-11/blob/main/nfc-app/MECHANICS.md). It describes any card mechanic three ways: its scale (one person and one card, up to many people on a trail of cards), its direction (the card only sends; the system behind it can answer back), and what a card can carry.
3. **Design.** With your partner, sketch a game, or one mechanic for a game, where the tap is the move. Where are the cards? Who taps them, and when? What happens after a tap?
4. **Test the reason.** Take the cards away. If your game still works just as well, the device isn't motivated yet. Change it until the card matters.
5. **To build it later:** [nfc-tools](https://github.com/tdm155ai/tdm155ai-week-11/blob/main/nfc-tools/README.md) writes a link onto a card, and [nfc-app](https://github.com/tdm155ai/tdm155ai-week-11/blob/main/nfc-app/README.md) is where the taps land.

## Bring back

- Your game or mechanic in one sentence.
- A sketch: the cards, the people, and what happens when someone taps.
- Why it needs the card.
