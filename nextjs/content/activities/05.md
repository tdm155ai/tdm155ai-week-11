Most of what you make won't be a game: presentations, pitches, papers, lab reports, research tools. Games can still lend them something. Read [Why?](/why) for the argument.

**You need:** a laptop with Claude, and something you have to make this term or next, real or imagined.

## Do

1. **Pick the thing you have to make.** A presentation, a pitch, a paper, a lab report, a tool for a lab, an essay.
2. **Pick one element of games.** From the mechanics you've seen today, or from the [list](/elements/mechanics). A few to start from: a map you can move around, feedback on every move, a sense of progress, a choice that changes what comes next, a timer, a score.
3. **Put it to work.** What would that one element do for your audience? What could they explore, try, or find out for themselves that a fixed page or slide couldn't let them?
4. **Sketch it, then ask Claude.** Draw it on paper first. Then describe it to Claude and ask for a quick working version, or a plan for one.
5. **Check it against your audience.** Does the element help them understand, or is it only decoration?

## Bring back

- What you are making, and the one element you took from games.
- What it lets your audience do.
- If you want to keep going, this can become this week's [practice](/practice): one game-like mechanic, somewhere you need to make things.
