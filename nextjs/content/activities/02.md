A **mechanic** is something a player can do, and how the game answers. You already know a lot of them from the games you play; this activity puts names on them.

**You need:** a laptop with Claude, a partner, and the [list of mechanics](/elements/mechanics).

## Do

1. **Pick a game you play a lot.** Any kind: a video game, a phone game, a board game, a card game, a sport.
2. **Ask Claude to map its mechanics.** Paste this, with your game's name in place of the brackets:

   > I play [GAME] a lot. Map out all of its game mechanics: everything the player can do, and how the game answers each action. Give one concrete example of each from the game. Group them, and then tell me which two or three mechanics matter most to how the game feels to play, and why. Use plain language, and tell me when you are unsure about a detail of the game.

3. **Check it.** You know this game better than Claude does. Cross out anything wrong, and add what it missed.
4. **Find the names.** Look through the [list of mechanics](/elements/mechanics). Which of them does your game use?
5. **Talk it over with your partner.** Explain your game's two or three key mechanics to someone who may not know it. Then listen to theirs. Do your two games share a mechanic?

## Bring back

- Your game, and the one mechanic it couldn't do without.
- One thing Claude got wrong or missed.
- A mechanic your game and your partner's game share.
