How to read
these numbers

Size is only the first question. These totals cover different periods and different revenue streams. They are nominal US dollars, before inflation adjustment. Disclosed means a company or official source states the figure; Reported means a publication or industry database reports it; Estimate means a modeled, approximate or incompletely verified figure. Hatching marks estimates. No bar measures profit.

A game’s ongoing spending, a film’s box office and a tour’s ticket sales all measure demand. Their accounting boundaries do not line up.

The bet

What does
it cost?

Development, production, marketing and ongoing support are separate buckets. A budget comparison needs to say which buckets it includes.

GTA VI

Budget: undisclosed.

Widely circulated billion-dollar estimates are not a company budget. GamesRadar reports that Rockstar disputed the $1.5B rumor[8]. An unknown total gets no bar.

Do not subtract one chart from another. These production costs exclude some costs and cover different periods. Gross receipts elsewhere on this page are not the producer’s net receipts. A big revenue total alone cannot establish profitability.

The design of the business

One launch.
Many ways
to keep earning.

The product’s design shapes when people pay—and what work must continue after release.

Games / the long tail

Purchase → play → updates → return

Take-Two’s filing describes GTA Online access, GTA+ membership and continuing content. GTA V has sold-in >230M units[2] as reported in November 2026[2]. That is neither unique people nor dollars.

Film / many windows

Cinema → home viewing → licensing

The box-office figures[12] count cinema tickets. Home entertainment and television licensing sit outside that measure. A film can keep earning after its theatrical run; box office alone cannot capture its full life.

Tour / repeat the event

Travel → perform → sell the next night

Pollstar’s full-tour estimate covers 149 shows[14] and 10.055M tickets[14]. The ticket gross comes before staging, travel, venue and other costs. Merchandising is a different stream.

Music / access & rights

Subscribe → listen → allocate royalties

Spotify describes payments to rights holders based on streamshare[17], rather than a fixed payment for each play. An artist’s contract then affects their share. Album-equivalent units are not a disclosed album revenue total.

A possible investment logic: repeated spending can spread a large initial cost across a longer period. That is an interpretation of the model, not proof that a particular budget will pay off. Ongoing content and service also cost money.

Read the number

Before you
repeat a
headline.

Gross is not profit.

Ask who receives the money and which expenses remain. Ticket gross is not a studio’s or performer’s take-home pay. Do not apply one universal cinema or platform percentage: terms vary by market, contract and product.

Units are not dollars.

Sell-in, sell-through, subscriptions, downloads, unique players and album-equivalent units count different things. Multiplying lifetime copies by today’s list price ignores discounts, editions and bundles.

Estimated is not disclosed.

A company can itself disclose an estimate, as Take-Two did for GTA V’s opening sales[3]. The source label tells you who is speaking; the measurement note tells you what uncertainty remains.

Time changes the comparison.

Lifetime income, annual market revenue and preorder snapshots have different clocks. Nominal dollars from different years do not buy the same amount. No inflation adjustment is applied here.

Popularity is not artistic value.

Receipts describe commercial demand, not the quality of a story or the experience of playing. Use them to ask about risk, access and creative freedom, rather than treating a sales ranking as a canon.

For discussion

What would
you measure?

  1. Which product comparison is most useful here? Which is most misleading?
  2. What additional evidence would you need before calling a release profitable?
  3. What kinds of mechanics encourage people to return? When does that serve the player, and when does it serve the business?
  4. How might a continuing-service model change a creator’s freedom—or obligations?
  5. What disappears when you compare a game, a film, an album and a concert only in dollars?
  6. If you built a media-economy dashboard, which definition would you put next to every number?
