# Game mechanics

*50 mechanics in 8 families. TDM 155AI, week 11.*

## Goals and the loop

**Goal and win condition.** The state that ends the game and says who won.

**Core loop.** The short cycle of actions the player repeats all game.

**Score and points.** A number that turns actions into progress you can compare.

**Lives, failure, and retry.** A way to lose that sends you back without ending play.

**Debt and obligation.** You owe something and cannot leave until it is paid.

## Time

**Timer and countdown.** A clock that forces a decision before it runs out.

**Turn order.** Players act one at a time in a set sequence.

**Real-time pressure.** Everyone acts at once and the world does not wait.

**Rhythm and timing window.** Act on the beat; how close you are is the score.

**Incremental growth.** Numbers grow by themselves, and the player tunes the rate.

**Escalation.** Each round asks a little more than the last.

## Chance and information

**Chance.** Dice, cards, or coins decide part of what happens.

**Push your luck.** Keep going for more, or stop and keep what you have.

**Hidden information.** Some players know what others do not.

**Hidden role and social deduction.** Secret roles; the group must work out who is who.

**Hidden rule.** One player invents a secret rule; the others discover it by playing.

**Fog of war and exploration.** The map is hidden until you go there.

**Asymmetric information.** Each group holds a part the others need.

## Players together

**Voting and the collective decision.** The group chooses, and everyone lives with the result.

**Cooperation.** Everyone wins or loses together.

**Roles and role swap.** Each player has a different power, and the roles can change hands.

**Trading and negotiation.** Players exchange what they have for what they need.

**Alliance and betrayal.** Deals that can be broken, with nothing to enforce them.

**The spect-actor.** Watchers can step in and change the play.

**Story circle.** Everyone adds to the story in turn.

**Mirroring and imitation.** Copy what the leader does.

## Resources and economy

**Resource management.** Spend limited things to get what you want.

**Engine building.** Early purchases make later turns stronger.

**Tech tree and unlocks.** Progress opens new options in a set order.

**Territory and area control.** Hold more of the board than the others.

**Worker placement.** Each player sends a few workers to claim scarce actions.

**Deck building and the draw.** Players build the deck they will draw from.

**Crafting and combining.** Put two things together to make a third.

## Space and the body

**Movement and position.** Where your body is changes what you can do.

**Zones and spatial triggers.** Stepping into a part of the room makes something happen.

**Gesture input.** The body is the controller.

**Memory and sequence.** Repeat a growing sequence without a mistake.

**Sensory constraint.** Take away a sense and the game changes.

**Location play.** The real world is the board.

## Rules that bend

**Constraint (the forbidden move).** A rule takes something away, and play has to work around it.

**Interruption and event cards.** Something from outside breaks in and must be absorbed.

**Rewind and do-over.** Go back and play the last moment again, changed.

**Irreversible choice.** A decision that cannot be undone shapes the rest of the game.

**Puzzle and lock-and-key.** A problem with a solution that opens the next part.

## Story, characters, and progress

**Branching dialogue.** The player picks what to say, and the conversation forks.

**The improvising character (AI co-star).** A character who answers whatever you say, in role.

**Quests and missions.** A task with a reward gives play a direction.

**Levels and progression.** Experience adds up to new abilities.

**Achievements and badges.** Named rewards for specific feats, collected and shown.

**Feedback ("juice").** Every action gets a sound, light, or shake that makes it feel good.
